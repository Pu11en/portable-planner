# What “Effective” Means

The planner is effective only when it improves the human experience and produces complete durable work. Artifact completeness alone is insufficient.

## 1. Orientation

**Pass:** After the opening, the user can tell what is being planned, what this session will produce, and that final execution is separate. Current and next steps use ordinary project language.

**Fail signals:** “What session is this?”, “What does this mean?”, confusing the sample plan with plugin implementation, unexplained process jargon, or a fixed progress count before a reliable map exists.

## 2. Question value

**Pass:** Every question changes a meaningful preference or direction decision with at least two viable answers. The agent derives obvious mechanics and researches facts.

**Fail signals:** obvious, repeated, factual, technical-routing, ceremonial, or already-answered questions; choices with only one sensible answer; or answer choices shown as numbers or unlabeled bullets instead of stable `A/B/C/D` keys.

## 3. Low reading load

**Pass:** Ordinary replies aim near 60 words and remain under 100, ask at most one question, use one concrete example for abstract choices, and show only the current and next step. Do not cut useful context merely to hit the target.

**Fail signals:** wall of text, multiple questions, long option explanations, repeated file/status narration, or abstract labels without an example.

## 4. Momentum without gaps

**Pass:** A simple plan finishes in one planning session when no real blocker exists. A complex plan expands only when research, prototype, size, or dependency evidence requires it. No major decision is skipped to create speed.

**Fail signals:** invented ceremony, premature multi-ticket maps, slow questioning, false progress counters, or a fast but incomplete plan.

## 5. Durable usable result

**Pass:** A fresh agent resumes from project files alone; the short overview and detailed decisions agree; execution tickets cover the whole route and can each be completed and verified in one fresh session.

**Fail signals:** reliance on chat memory, ambiguous next session, unresolved choices hidden in execution, oversized tickets, or duplicated contradictory state.

## 6. Human preference

**Pass:** Drew says the first ordinary and naturally complex real-use flows are faster and clearer than his current planning experience, the questions were worth answering, and he would actually use the plans and execution tickets.

Automated checks may reject a build, but they cannot supply this final pass.

## Real-use measurement

For every turn, log the exact visible reply, word count, question count, decision changed, files updated, confusion or repetition, and Drew's reaction. Any confusion turn is a failure until the very next reply restores orientation without asking another planning question.
