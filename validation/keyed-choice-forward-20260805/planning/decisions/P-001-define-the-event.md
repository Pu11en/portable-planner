# P-001 — Define the Event

- Status: current
- Depends on: none

## Decision

Define a cohesive neighborhood skill-swap day that prioritizes useful help exchanged between residents. The next decision is how much advance matching to use so the event produces tangible value without becoming too difficult to organize.

## Viable options

- Hybrid matching: collect offers and requests beforehand, arrange strong matches, and keep a drop-in board for the day; balances reliability and access, but needs both advance coordination and on-site facilitation.
- Pre-matched appointments: schedule exchanges before the event; gives participants the clearest expectation of receiving useful help, but creates the most administration and makes no-shows costly.
- Drop-in marketplace: let participants post offers and needs when they arrive; easiest to join and organize, but useful matches are less predictable.

## Recommendation

Use hybrid matching. It supports the confirmed practical-help priority while leaving room for residents who did not register early; the tradeoff is a moderate coordination burden before and during the event.

## Confirmed decision

- Primary outcome: prioritize practical help traded between neighbors, so attendees should leave having given or received useful help rather than merely socialized or watched demonstrations.
- Exchange structure: not confirmed.

## Evidence

- No external evidence needed; this is a human direction choice.

## Effects

- The practical-help priority makes successful exchanges, rather than attendance alone, the central measure of value.
- The exchange structure will determine the registration process, staffing, schedule, on-site tools, and resilience to no-shows or unmatched requests.
- Keep one planning ticket until the remaining event choices are cohesive; split only if a concrete research, prototype, dependency, or session-size limit appears.

## Complete when

The event's primary outcome, intended participants, exchange format, core flow, logistical assumptions, accessibility and safety boundaries, outreach approach, resource needs, and objective success measures are explicit and mutually consistent, and the plan is ready to be audited and converted into execution tickets.
