# P-001 — Design the neighborhood skill night

- Status: current
- Depends on: none

## Decision

Turn the monthly peer-teaching idea into a cohesive, launch-ready plan.

The primary experience is now settled: each attendee should leave having practiced one useful skill and made a few new neighbor connections. This keeps the first night useful and social without requiring a complicated multi-skill program.

The current unresolved choice is who the first night is designed for. That decision must come before the teaching format because it changes suitable skills, pacing, safety boundaries, venue needs, outreach, and attendance rules.

## Viable options

- A. Adults and older teens — recommended because it keeps the first night broadly welcoming while making pacing and safety simpler than an all-ages event.
- B. Adults only — simplest to design and supervise, but excludes younger residents and families who may want to participate together.
- C. Families and all ages — most inclusive, but requires child-safe skills, clearer supervision boundaries, and more flexible pacing.

## Recommendation

A — Design the first night for adults and older teens; it preserves a neighborhood-wide feel while keeping the pilot manageable.

## Confirmed decision

- Primary experience: each attendee leaves having practiced one useful skill and made a few new neighbor connections.
- Rationale: this balances usefulness and community with a simple first-night promise, accepting less variety in exchange for deeper participation and manageable complexity.

## Evidence

- No external evidence needed for this human preference.

## Effects

- The event must include hands-on practice rather than demonstrations alone.
- The route must protect enough time for one skill to be practiced and for low-pressure neighbor introductions or conversation.
- Teacher recruitment, venue setup, and outreach must support a clear one-skill-per-attendee promise rather than a sampler or ongoing matching service.
- Success proof must check both whether attendees practiced a useful skill and whether they made new neighbor connections.
- The intended audience remains unresolved and will determine suitable skills, pacing, safety boundaries, venue needs, outreach, and attendance rules.
- Keep the plan as one planning ticket unless a concrete research, prototype, size, or dependency limit appears.

## Complete when

The destination, boundaries, attendee experience, event route, ownership, safety needs, objective proof, and all downstream execution tickets agree, with no major planning question unresolved.
