# P-001 — Workshop Operating Model

- Status: complete
- Depends on: none

## Decision

Settle the complete operating model for a one-day, hands-on AI workshop so execution can book, build, recruit, prepare, deliver, and close the event without reopening a major planning choice.

## Viable options

- A. Paid pilot — charge $149 for 20 standard seats and reserve five full-scholarship seats; improves commitment and recovers up to $2,980, with a higher access barrier.
- B. Refundable deposit — charge $49 and return it after attendance; lowers the barrier and discourages no-shows, but creates refund administration and recovers no cost.
- C. Free application — widest access and simplest attendee payment experience, but creates more no-show exposure and no cost recovery.

## Recommendation

A — Use a paid pilot with five full scholarships. It balances attendance commitment, access, and partial cost recovery without making ticket revenue necessary to fund the event.

## Confirmed decision

The simulated profile answer is A. The operating model is:

- **Destination:** within eight weeks, run one local Saturday workshop from 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. for at most 25 small-business owners, led by Drew and two helpers.
- **Attendee result:** every attendee leaves with one AI-assisted business workflow that they personally ran at least twice with synthetic, redacted, or public sample data, that a facilitator reviewed, and that the attendee can repeat after the workshop.
- **Review standard:** the workflow card names its trigger, safe inputs, steps, intended output, human check, and next real-business use. A facilitator records pass or a corrected re-test against usefulness, repeatability, and data-safety checks.
- **Audience support:** mixed beginners bring laptops. Prerequisite instructions, an account/access check, a synthetic sample-data pack, paired build lanes, two helper clinics, and a tested no-confidential-data fallback prevent setup or skill gaps from consuming the build session.
- **Attendance model:** offer 20 standard seats at $149 and five full-scholarship seats. Cap confirmed attendance at 25 and maintain a waitlist. Offer a full refund through 14 days before the event and ticket transfer through 48 hours before it; refill released seats from the waitlist.
- **Budget:** authorize no more than $4,000 in cash commitments before revenue: venue/A/V $1,100; food and beverages $600; two helper stipends $900; print, supplies, and accessibility $300; insurance/permits $350; software, ticketing, and communications $300; contingency $450. Drew's time is not a cash expense. Reallocation is allowed inside the total only if safety, accessibility, helper coverage, and venue essentials remain funded; any commitment above $4,000 returns to planning.
- **Venue rule:** select a local, physically accessible room for 28 people (25 attendees plus three staff), with reliable internet verified by an on-site test, adequate power, projector/display, movable work tables, permitted food service, and written cancellation terms. Use the organizer's public business location as the search center and prefer a venue within 30 driving minutes. The final venue/date is an execution selection against these rules, not an unresolved plan preference.
- **Go/no-go:** at the 14-day checkpoint, record cohort readiness. If attendance is below 18, conduct five days of targeted waitlist/partner outreach; do not add unbudgeted spend. Recheck attendance at the seven-day final go/no-go. Hold the event only if at least 18 are then confirmed and the venue, internet fallback, three-person staff, approved-service list, and data-safety controls all pass. If any hard gate is false, pause new commitments and return to planning.
- **Agenda:** 9:00 welcome and safety setup; 9:30 choose and map one workflow; 10:15 break; 10:30 guided example and build setup; 11:15 build sprint one; 12:15 lunch; 1:00 build sprint two; 2:00 break; 2:15 safe-data testing and correction; 3:15 facilitator review clinics; 4:00 demonstrations and next-use commitments; 4:20 close and feedback; finish at 4:30.
- **Safety:** workshop exercises use only synthetic, redacted, or public data in services on the organizer's pre-published approved list. Never enter confidential customer, employee, payment, health, legal, credential, or proprietary data. If exposure is suspected, stop the workflow, close the service, preserve no copies, notify Drew privately, and record only non-sensitive incident facts for follow-up.
- **Proof:** retain the dated venue/agenda record, attendance count, one passed review checklist per attendee, anonymized completion totals, safety incident log (including zero incidents), feedback summary, and final expense ledger.
- **Closeout:** send attendee workflow cards and a safe-next-use reminder within two business days, settle expenses, report outcome and budget against the success checks, and record improvements without silently changing this plan.

## Evidence

- No external evidence was needed to select the operating model. Venue availability, actual quotes, local requirements, service terms, and connectivity are execution facts that must be verified directly before commitments.

## Effects

- Creates the sequential execution route E-001 through E-010.
- Makes E-001 the only initially eligible execution ticket after approval.
- Blocks spending above $4,000, use of unapproved services or confidential data, and workshop delivery without the dated go/no-go gates.
- Requires human review for venue/spending commitments, public registration, participant materials, final readiness, workshop-day evidence, and closeout.

## Complete when

Destination, result standard, attendance model, budget, venue criteria, agenda, safety, go/no-go rules, evidence, and closeout are explicit; execution tickets cover the route in dependency order; all canonical files agree; and the plan waits for explicit visual approval.
