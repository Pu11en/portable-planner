# P-001 — Define the Meetup's Primary Promise

- Status: current
- Depends on: none

## Decision

Settle the main result the monthly meetup should prioritize. This choice determines what happens in each session, why local business owners return, and what evidence would show the meetup is useful.

## Viable options

- Owners leave with one working AI improvement applied to their business. This produces concrete value, but requires guided work time and a tightly scoped session.
- Owners solve problems and learn from a trusted local peer community. This may strengthen retention and relationships, but the practical result will vary by participant.
- Owners build broad AI awareness through demonstrations and expert teaching. This is easy to attend and program, but risks passive learning with little business change.

For the chosen peer problem-solving priority, the viable recurring formats are:

- Small facilitated circles where every owner works on a current problem. Everyone participates, but facilitation must keep the groups focused.
- One live owner hot seat discussed by the whole group. The group can go deeper, but most participants mainly observe.
- An open roundtable where owners raise problems as they arise. This is flexible and social, but outcomes are less consistent.

## Recommendation

Within the confirmed peer problem-solving priority, use small facilitated circles. That lets every owner receive practical help while strengthening relationships, though it requires clear prompts and active facilitation.

## Confirmed decision

Prioritize practical problem-solving and learning within a trusted local peer group. The meetup should help owners work through real business problems together; it does not need to promise that every participant completes an implementation during the session.

## Evidence

- No external evidence needed for this direction choice.

## Effects

- The participant promise should center on useful peer input and a clearer next action for a real business problem.
- The recurring format must give owners a structured way to contribute and receive help rather than relying mainly on lectures or tool demonstrations.
- Success evidence should capture whether owners received useful input, left with a clear next action, and would return to the group.
- Keep this as one planning ticket unless research, a prototype, or session size creates a concrete reason to split the work.

## Complete when

The primary priority is confirmed and the participant promise, pilot format, first-event scope, proof of value, and resulting execution route all agree with it.
