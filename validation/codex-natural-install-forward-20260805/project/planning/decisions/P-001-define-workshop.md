# P-001 — Define the workshop and complete the plan

- Status: current
- Depends on: none

## Decision

Define a safe, workable first workshop and turn it into a complete plan. The delivery model and task-scope model are now settled. The current decision is which task families the first workshop will name on its short supported menu; this must be clear before capacity, staffing, registration, and the two-hour flow can be fixed honestly.

## Viable options

- A. Everyday essentials — Recommended; cover email and attachments, photos and files, video calls, online forms and uploads, and basic device or accessibility settings. Best match for mixed needs, but helpers prepare across five task families.
- B. Communication and sharing — Cover email and attachments, messaging, video calls, and sharing photos. Easiest menu to staff safely, but leaves out common online life-admin tasks.
- C. Forms and online services — Cover online forms, appointment or service portals, library sites, and uploads or downloads. Highly practical, but creates more account, privacy, and site-variation contingencies.

## Recommendation

A — Use the everyday essentials menu. It gives adults with mixed goals a meaningful chance to recognize their task while keeping the advertised scope to five preparable families. Screened custom requests remain available under the confirmed boundary.

## Confirmed decision

Use a capped coaching workshop. Participants register in advance with one intended task and device information, and only confirmed participants attend. This model was selected because it gives each person the strongest chance to practice and then repeat their task independently within two hours. Its accepted tradeoff is lower attendance than a group lab or open clinic.

Use a short supported menu plus screened custom requests. Registration will show a limited set of task families that staff can prepare for, while still allowing a participant to submit a different everyday digital task for review. A custom request is accepted only when staff can prepare for it, coach it safely on the participant's own device, and leave enough session time for the participant to repeat it independently. The choice preserves meaningful participant choice while accepting that some requests will be redirected or deferred before the workshop.

## Evidence

- No external evidence needed for this direction choice.

## Effects

- Registration is required and must collect the intended task, device type, and accessibility or accommodation needs before a place is confirmed.
- The workshop will have a fixed attendance cap and planned helper coverage; the exact cap and ratio remain open until the supported menu is settled.
- Staff screen requests before the event, confirm supported tasks, and redirect unsafe or out-of-scope requests rather than discovering them during the session.
- Requests are not confirmed when they require staff to handle passwords, make financial, medical, or legal decisions or transactions, bypass account or device security, repair a compromised device, or complete the task on the participant's behalf.
- The supported menu must be short and concrete enough for registrants to recognize their task and for helpers to prepare; its task families are the next decision.
- The room flow must include individual coaching space followed by an independent repeat or teach-back, not only a shared demonstration.
- Success is participant-level: each attendee safely repeats the chosen task on their own device with no step-by-step prompting, or leaves with a clearly documented next step when a pre-agreed contingency applies.
- The remaining route is: choose the supported menu; derive capacity and staffing; define registration and the two-hour flow; close safety, accessibility, and contingency gaps; then create the run-ready execution tickets and visual approval surface.

## Complete when

All material workshop decisions and their effects are explicit, the plan has no major contradiction or unsafe gap, the visual route is ready for review, and the next action is exact.
