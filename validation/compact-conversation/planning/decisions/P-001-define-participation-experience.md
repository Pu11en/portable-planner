# P-001 — Define the Participation Experience

- Status: complete
- Depends on: none

## Decision

Set how guided participation should feel, then turn that choice into a complete event flow. This determines whether shy newcomers can join comfortably without making the night feel like generic networking.

## Viable options

- A. Guided small-group rounds — Begin with an easy paired warm-up, then use timed skill rounds in small groups. Most supportive for first-timers, but less free-form.
- B. Two featured mini-lessons — Everyone follows the same two volunteer-led activities. Clearest and easiest to host, but fewer people teach each night.
- C. Open skill stations — Attendees choose among informal activity tables and move when they like. Most flexible, but shy newcomers must initiate more interactions.

## Recommendation

A — Guided small-group rounds. The extra structure gives first-time attendees an obvious way to begin and keeps the event focused on doing, with less room for it to drift into networking.

## Confirmed decision

Use guided small-group rounds:

- Arrange three practical-skill stations led by three volunteer teachers. Teachers stay at their stations while three small learner groups rotate through every station.
- Use this 90-minute flow: welcome and paired warm-up (0–10), explain the stations and group routes (10–15), three 20-minute skill rounds (15–75), close and collect the two-question success check (75–85), then reset the free library room (85–90).
- Let attendees choose a learner or teacher role before the event. No first-time attendee must teach, introduce themselves to the full room, or initiate an unstructured conversation.
- Give every attendee an immediate arrival instruction, a partner for the warm-up, and an assigned first station. Allow anyone to observe or pass on an activity.
- Teachers describe one small result achievable in 20 minutes and state what participants must bring. The organizer supplies no paid activity materials.
- Keep the focus on practicing skills. Do not add pitches, open mingling, or generic networking exercises.
- Ask whether each respondent is attending for the first time, whether they felt comfortable joining in, and whether they would return. Review results after every event.

## Evidence

- No external evidence needed; this is a human preference within the fixed event constraints.

## Effects

- The decision creates five execution steps: prepare the reusable event kit, open sign-up, lock the stations and group routes, run the guided event, then review the results and adjust the next month.

## Complete when

Complete. The participation format, full 90-minute flow, newcomer supports, participant and host responsibilities, recurring setup, and success check are explicit; all canonical files agree; and the complete route is covered by session-sized execution tickets.
